Confirmed from floor plan: the maid room has a direct door opening onto the building's shared lobby / lift corridor — staff access does not require transit through the private bedroom zone. Maid Room — No Natural Daylighting · DBC B.6.5.2.1 FAIL DBC Fail
The maid room is an internal room with no external window. DBC 2021 B.6.5.2.1 explicitly states: "Housekeeper's rooms shall be provided with adequate daylighting similar to living spaces as shown in Table B.8." Table B.8 requires a minimum window glazing area of 10% of the room floor area. For this maid room (8.4m²) → minimum glazed window area = 0.84m². No window = no daylighting = mandatory DBC violation. Note: DBC H.4.11 confirms that natural ventilation alone is never relied upon in Dubai — HVAC is mandatory regardless. The fail here is specifically about natural light (daylighting), which cannot be replaced by mechanical systems. Recommendation: Coordinate with architect and façade engineer to introduce a window to an external wall or light well. If structurally impossible, a skylight or borrowed light solution requires DM pre-approval before authority submission.
Guest Powder Room — Confirmed at Internal Lobby Pass
Confirmed from floor plan (June 2026 review): a dedicated guest WC is positioned directly off the internal unit lobby (LOB.) — accessible from the entry sequence before reaching the living/dining zone. This is a correct and considered placement for a branded residence: guests pass through the lobby, powder room access is discreet without requiring entry deeper into the unit. The lobby-adjacent position also separates it from the bedroom zone. Adjacency to a store room confirms the service core logic is resolved. DBC B.8.1 satisfied (min 1 bathroom per unit confirmed beyond this).

Living / Dining Analysis

The open-plan living, dining, and kitchen zone is the signature space of this unit. At 100.5m², it represents the primary investment thesis for a buyer — this is where The Chedi brand promise is spatially expressed or broken.

Living Zone Width
8,500 mm
Across entertaining zone

At 8.5m, the living zone width allows simultaneous formal dining, lounge seating, and informal sitting without overlap. Minimum recommended for this typology is 4,000mm — this unit clears it by more than double.

Living Zone Depth
11,820 mm
Front balcony wall to kitchen zone

An 11.82m depth gives exceptional layering potential: formal dining near balcony, transition zone, TV lounge, kitchen peninsula — all in a flowing, uninterrupted open plan. GCC benchmark for luxury living depth is 4,000mm minimum; this exceeds by nearly 3×.

Direct Balcony Connection from Living Zone Pass
The main living zone connects directly to the top balcony (≈33.8m²), creating an indoor-outdoor living continuum critical on Sheikh Zayed Road. This is the primary view axis and the plan exploits it correctly — the living zone is oriented towards the balcony connection rather than pointing away from it.
Multiple Entertainment Zones — Enabled Pass
The 8,500mm width × 11,820mm depth format allows the interior designer to program at least three distinct zones within the open plan: formal dining (8-10 pax), primary lounge, and secondary sitting or library zone. This is the correct spatial programming response for a Chedi-branded product targeting HNW owner-occupiers.
SZR West-Facing Solar Exposure — Design Consideration Note
Sheikh Zayed Road runs roughly north-south. Units facing west (typical for towers on the eastern side of the road) receive intense afternoon solar gain in the UAE climate. The main balcony and living zone's direct solar orientation will require high-performance glazing (SHGC ≤ 0.25), automated blinds, or deep overhangs. This is a design management issue, not a planning failure — but it must be addressed in the façade specification.

Main Weaknesses Ranked by Severity

Issues ordered by impact on buyer perception, DBC compliance risk, and construction-stage remediation difficulty. Red items require immediate design response. Amber items are manageable within the current scheme with targeted adjustments.

#1 · Secondary Bedroom Widths Below GCC Luxury Standard
3,095mm
Bedrooms 01 and 02 are DBC compliant (3,000mm min met) but fall below the GCC luxury standard of 3,500mm minimum width. In the context of a Chedi-branded product starting from 3,556 sqft, buyers purchasing at this price point will compare to Four Seasons Residences, BVLGARI Residences, and Dorchester Collection where secondary bedrooms are consistently 3,500–4,200mm wide. Recommendation: Review structural grid for possibility of widening both rooms by 250–400mm by reducing the dressing room width or redistributing the corridor allocation.
#2 · Guest Powder Room — Confirmed at Lobby · Correct Placement
Confirmed
Guest powder room confirmed from floor plan review: dedicated WC positioned off the internal unit lobby (LOB.), directly adjacent to the store room — forming a clean service core at the unit entry. This resolves the previous flag. The lobby-facing position is correct for a luxury branded residence: guests access the powder room discreetly before entering the living zone, without transiting through the bedroom corridor. This is consistent with 5-star hospitality planning logic. Previous flag withdrawn — no programming gap.
#3 · Bedroom Corridor at 1,100mm — Below GCC Best Practice
−100mm
The 1,100mm corridor connecting the lobby to all bedrooms and maid room is DBC compliant (≥1,000mm) but fails GCC best practice (1,200mm) and is not suitable for wheelchair access (1,200mm min) or two-person passing with luggage. Recommendation: Coordinate structural feasibility for 100mm widening. If structural walls prevent this, ensure corridor is at least activated with art niches or wall lighting to reduce its perceived narrowness.
#4 · Maid Room — No Natural Daylighting · DBC 2021 B.6.5.2.1 FAIL
DBC Fail
The maid room has no external window. DBC 2021 B.6.5.2.1 explicitly requires housekeeper's rooms to have natural daylighting equivalent to living spaces, per Table B.8: minimum glazed window area = 10% of room floor area. For this maid room (8.4m²) → minimum required glazed area = 0.84m². No window = no daylighting = mandatory DBC violation. Note: DBC H.4.11 confirms mechanical HVAC is mandatory in Dubai regardless — natural ventilation cannot be relied upon and is not the issue here. This failure is specifically about natural light, which cannot be replaced by any mechanical system. This will not pass Dubai Municipality authority submission. Recommendation: Introduce a window to an external wall or light well. If structurally impossible, a compliant borrowed-light solution or skylight requires DM pre-approval before submission.
#5 · SZR West-Facing Solar Load on Primary Balcony
Façade Risk
The primary balcony and living zone direct view toward Sheikh Zayed Road. Depending on tower orientation (west-facing = afternoon sun in UAE), the glazing and balcony without confirmed shading elements will result in significant thermal discomfort and energy load. Recommendation: Confirm solar orientation with MEP team. Specify façade SHGC ≤ 0.25, automated external blinds on balcony glazing, and review whether the balcony soffit provides adequate overhang depth for summer equinox sun angles.
#6 · Linen / Utility Closet Not Confirmed in Bedroom Zone
Minor Gap
A linen closet or utility cupboard within the bedroom corridor is not identifiable in the detail plan provided. For a 3BR + Maid unit, housekeeping without a centralized linen store requires cross-unit movement with linens. Recommendation: Introduce a min. 1.0m² linen cupboard within the bedroom corridor. This is typically achievable by adjusting corridor termination at the lobby zone.

Qualified Luxury

The Chedi Sanctuary 3BR + Maid unit delivers an exceptionally strong living and entertaining proposition. The open-plan living/dining/kitchen at 100.5m² and a master bedroom suite at 49.5m² combined (bedroom + ensuite) are among the strongest spatial offers in the 3BR branded residence segment on SZR. The 33.8m² primary balcony reinforces the outdoor-living positioning fundamental to The Chedi brand identity.

However, the unit has two material weaknesses for its price tier: secondary bedrooms at 3,095–3,200mm width that clear DBC but fall below the GCC luxury standard of 3,500mm, and an unconfirmed guest powder room in the public zone — a functional gap that reads poorly against comparable branded products. These are pre-construction-stage issues, meaning they are still resolvable through minor structural-grid adjustments if actioned now.

The corridor at 1,100mm and the maid room positioning represent lower-tier concerns that can be managed through interior design without structural change. The solar orientation risk is a façade engineering issue, not a planning failure.

Professional Recommendation: This unit is a strong product with a commercially viable layout. Before sales launch, three items require developer action: (1) confirm or introduce a guest powder room in the public zone; (2) review feasibility of widening secondary bedrooms by 250–400mm; (3) brief the MEP and façade team on the solar load mitigation strategy for the primary balcony elevation. These are pre-design-freeze corrections — cost-of-change is low now and prohibitive post-contract.
