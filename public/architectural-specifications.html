035440
Cast Underlayment Screeds
The levelling layer poured over the structural slab to create a flat, accurate base for all floor finishes — tiles, stone, timber, or vinyl.
▾
What It Is

A cement-sand mortar layer laid on the structural concrete slab. Its job is purely functional: create the correct floor level and a smooth, bonded surface for the final finish. There are three types used on this project: standard aggregate screed, lightweight screed (for roof/balcony areas where dead load matters), and aerated screed (interior only).

Key Technical Requirements
Compressive StrengthMin 20 MPa at 28 days (ASTM C109M)
Max Density12 kN/m²
Water/Cement RatioMax 0.45
Max Slump125 mm
Min Cement:Sand Ratio1:4 by volume
Thickness > 70mmSteel reinforcement required
Application Temp10°C – 30°C ambient and slab surface
CuringContinuous moisture minimum 7 days
Flatness ToleranceMax 6 mm gap under 3m straightedge
Fibre ReinforcementMin 0.90 kg/m³ polypropylene fibre
Submittals Required Before Work
  • Product data and technical datasheets for all materials
  • Design mix with 28-day compressive strength data
  • Material test reports from independent testing agency
  • Manufacturer certificates for cement, aggregates, admixtures
  • Mockup of 9.3 m² — approved by Engineer before mass pour
Site Technical Architect — Supervision Checkpoints
  • Verify slab is dry: plastic sheet test (ASTM D4263) before application
  • Confirm ambient and slab temperature is within 10°C–30°C range
  • Witness bonding slurry application (1 part cement : 2 parts sand + acrylic agent)
  • Check pour is continuous — no cold joints within a strip
  • Test surface flatness with 3m straightedge — reject if gap >6mm
  • Sample sets: 3 cube specimens per 93 m² (min 6/day) tested at 28 days
  • Chain-drag test for delamination before floor finish begins
  • No construction traffic for 28 days after placement
Critical: Do not allow floor finishes to commence until screed achieves 28-day strength and passes chain-drag delamination test. Any failure = remove and replace at contractor's cost.

044200
Exterior Stone Cladding
Natural stone panels fixed to the building's outer face on a metal grid system — the premium exterior skin that defines the building's appearance and must resist wind, seismic load, and UAE weather for the building's lifetime.
▾
What It Is

Stone panels mechanically anchored (not glued) to a steel sub-frame attached to the building structure. Includes stone trim: bands, copings, sills, jambs, and soffits. All anchors, sub-frame, sealant joints, and sheathing are part of this system — not just the visible stone face.

Key Technical Requirements
Anchor StrengthMin 2× weight of stone in compression AND tension (ASTM C1242)
Stone SourceSingle quarry per variety — no mixing of sources
Temp MovementAllow for 67°C ambient / 100°C surface change
Interstory DriftFloor height ÷ 400 max horizontal movement
Anchor Safety FactorDesign ultimate strength ÷ safety factor per ASTM C1242
Hot WeatherComply with TMS 602/ACI 530.1 — cover tops daily
Submittals Required Before Work
  • Stone samples: min 5 pieces of 300×300mm per variety (showing full colour range)
  • Shop drawings signed by PE: anchor locations, joint details, metal grid layout
  • Stone test reports: flexural strength wet & dry (ASTM C880), anchorage (ASTM C1354)
  • Anchoring system lab mockup test: 3× design load (ASTM C1201, Procedure B)
  • Sealant compatibility test from joint sealant manufacturer
  • Welding procedure and personnel qualifications (AWS D1.1, D1.6)
Site Technical Architect — Supervision Checkpoints
  • Verify stone delivery matches approved sample set — reject any unit outside approved range
  • Do not allow sealant installation below 5°C or when substrate is wet
  • Check anchor type: interior = zinc-plated carbon steel; exterior = SS316 only
  • Confirm each stone unit is marked with shop drawing designation before installation
  • Verify veining/grain direction matches approved sequence on shop drawing
  • Inspect mockup of typical exterior wall panel including window return before mass installation
  • Protect stone tops daily with non-staining waterproof sheeting (extend 600mm down)
Never substitute stone variety or source without formal Engineer approval. Different quarry batches create visible colour banding that cannot be corrected after installation.

Waterproofing Systems
The watertight barrier protecting all below-ground, wet-area, and roof surfaces. Three technologies are used: bituminous sheet (torch-applied), cold fluid-applied membrane, and polymer-modified cement — each applied in the right location for the right conditions.
▾
System Types & Locations
071351 Bituminous SheetBelow-grade slabs, basement walls, podium deck — torch-applied SBS/APP modified
071416 Cold FluidWet rooms, planters, balconies — brush/roller applied acrylic or polyurethane
071613 Polymer CementWet areas with tile finish — crystalline or cementitious slurry coat
Critical Rules for All Waterproofing
SubstrateDry, clean, free of fins, voids, and honeycombing before application
Single SourceAll components from one manufacturer — no mixing of brands
UpstandsMin 150mm above finished floor level at all perimeters
CuringNo subsequent work on membrane until manufacturer's cure time confirmed
Site Supervision Checkpoints
  • Witness substrate preparation and primer application before membrane — photograph
  • Check membrane thickness with wet-film gauge during application
  • Inspect all laps, corners, pipe penetrations, and drain sumps — these are the failure points
  • Raise IR before any screed or tile covers the waterproofing — it must be inspected open
  • Flood test all wet rooms: 24–48 hours minimum, 50mm water depth — witness and sign off
  • Pressure hose test any area that cannot be flood tested
Any waterproofing covered without an approved IR is a buried unknown defect. If a leak appears post-handover, the contractor has no defence. Enforce the inspection hold point with zero exceptions.

Elastomeric Sheet Waterproofing
Integrally bonded HDPE pre-applied (blind-side) waterproofing membrane installed against the soil side of below-grade retaining structures before concrete is cast. Primary groundwater barrier for the life of the building — no post-construction access possible.
▾
Part 1 — Scope

Waterproofing systems designed to prevent water migration around below-grade structures. Specifically: integrally bonded HDPE sheet for pre-applied or blind-side applications installed before concrete is cast against them. Membranes separated from concrete by protection layers (protection boards, sand-cement screed) are not acceptable under this section — integral bond is the design intent.

Part 1 — Preinstallation Conference
  • Review waterproofing requirements: surface preparation, substrate condition, pretreatment, minimum curing period before membrane, drainage layers
  • Review environmental conditions: temperature, humidity, wind, UAE summer heat effects on adhesion
  • Review interface with Sections 033713 Shotcrete and 033000 CIP Concrete — substrate quality requirements
  • Review penetration details: MEP sleeves, drain outlets, pile head junctions, day joints
  • Review inspection hold points and photographic documentation requirements
Part 1 — Submittals
  • Product data: construction details, material descriptions, tested physical and performance properties
  • Manufacturer's written instructions for substrate evaluation, preparation, and pretreatment
  • Applicator qualification evidence — manufacturer-approved installer required; no substitution
  • Shop drawings: penetrations, corners, laps, pile-head junctions, day-joint treatment, transitions
  • Warranty: manufacturer's written warranty for waterproofing system
  • Compatibility certification: between membrane and concrete mix design used on project
Part 2 — System Requirements
Membrane TypeIntegrally bonded HDPE sheet — pre-applied or blind-side system
Bond MechanismBonds directly to fresh concrete as it is cast — no separation layer permitted
Protection BoardsNOT permitted — any physical separation between membrane and structural concrete voids the system
Application ZonesSoil face of secant pile walls, underside of pile caps, raft slab soffit, any below-grade water-retaining zone
Related SectionsSec. 033713 Shotcrete and Sec. 033000 CIP Concrete govern substrate quality requirements
Part 3 — Execution
SubstrateClean, sound, free from voids, honeycombs, protrusions, and contamination — Engineer-approved before membrane starts
LapsMinimum overlap per manufacturer — heat- or adhesive-sealed throughout length — 100% inspection required
PenetrationsAll MEP sleeves, drainage pipes, pile head junctions: properly detailed with membrane collars and sealant — no unsealed penetrations
Day JointsAreas where membrane work stopped: confirm continuity of bond before resumption — inspect and re-seal as required
Foot TrafficProtective walk boards required over laid membrane — no direct foot traffic, no dropping of sharp objects

Photo RecordComprehensive photographic record of all membrane zones before concrete permanently covers them
Site Technical Architect — Supervision Checkpoints
  • Verify installer is manufacturer-approved — do not accept unapproved applicator on site
  • Confirm shotcrete/concrete substrate approved by Engineer before membrane work begins
  • Inspect all membrane laps minimum 100% — no gaps, no partial seals, no lifted edges
  • Check every penetration detail is complete and sealed — no open sleeves covered by membrane without proper collar treatment
  • Issue WIR hold point and physically inspect before signing off — never delegate to Contractor
  • Conduct and record photographic survey of every zone before pour — date/time stamped
  • Mark any suspect area — repair and re-inspect before concrete proceeds over that area
This is a life-of-building decision. Once concrete is cast against this membrane, no inspection, repair, or replacement is possible without demolition. The WIR hold point before concrete placement is mandatory and non-delegatable. Any area with suspected damage must be confirmed repaired before concrete proceeds — no exceptions.

Site Clearing
Removal of all above-grade obstructions within the enabling works footprint: structures, vegetation, debris, and surface slabs. First enabling works activity — must be complete before piling rigs or bulk excavation equipment mobilise.
▾
Part 1 — Scope

Site clearing encompasses stripping topsoil and vegetation, grubbing roots and stumps, demolishing existing surface structures and slabs within the clearing limits, and removing all cleared material to approved disposal points. Legally defined tree and plant protection zones must be established before any work begins. This section is the first activity and gates all subsequent enabling works.

Part 1 — Preinstallation Conference
  • Review demolition methodology and sequence
  • Review existing utility locations — confirm Miss Utility/RTA notification completed
  • Review tree and plant protection zone boundaries and enforcement procedures
  • Review haul routes: DM and RTA permit status
  • Review erosion and sedimentation control measures before stripping begins
  • Confirm disposal site approvals
Part 1 — Submittals
  • Pre-clearing photographic/video survey of full site and all adjoining structures
  • Haul route plan with DM/RTA permit numbers
  • Utility locator notification confirmation
  • Tree and plant protection zone plan — Engineer-approved before work starts
  • Erosion and sedimentation control plan
  • Demolition methodology statement
Part 2 / Part 3 — Scope Items and Protection Zones
StrippingRemove topsoil and vegetation across full enabling works footprint to limits shown on drawings
GrubbingRemove roots and stumps — grub to depth sufficient to prevent regrowth
DemolitionExisting surface slabs, kerbs, paving, and structures within footprint — break out and remove

  • No water impoundment or ponding of any kind
  • No excavation or other ground disturbance without Engineer's explicit written direction

312319
Dewatering
Design, installation, continuous operation, and monitoring of the groundwater control system. Piezometric level maintained minimum 800mm below active excavation bottom at all times. Delegated PE design, continuous operation, full standby capacity, and daily monitoring logs are all mandatory.
▾
Part 1 — Scope

Construction dewatering: design, furnish, install, test, operate, monitor, and maintain a dewatering system of sufficient capacity to maintain the water table minimum 800mm below the active excavation bottom throughout the entire construction sequence. System must operate continuously without interruption. Related: Section 312000 Earth Moving for coordinating surface-water runoff control.

Part 1 — Preinstallation Conference
  • Verify availability of installer's personnel, equipment, and facilities
  • Review site conditions and coordination with temporary erosion control
  • Review geotechnical report — groundwater levels and permeability data
  • Review proposed site clearing and excavation stages
  • Review existing utilities and subsurface conditions
  • Review observation well and piezometer layout and monitoring plan
Part 1 — Submittals
  • Delegated PE design — signed and sealed: plans, elevations, sections, details
  • Layout showing well/well-point locations, risers, headers, filters, pumps, power supply, standby equipment
  • Piezometer and flow-measuring device layout for monitoring performance
  • Written dewatering operation plan — sequence of well placement coordinated with excavation stages
  • Delegated design data signed and sealed by qualified PE
  • Field quality-control reports (ongoing)
  • Installer and land surveyor qualifications
  • Pre-dewatering existing conditions photographs/video of adjacent construction
  • Record drawings at completion: locations, depths of capped wells and abandoned equipment
Part 1 — Field Conditions
Geotechnical ReportAvailable for information only — Contractor makes additional borings if needed at own cost
Survey RequirementQualified land surveyor surveys adjacent buildings, structures, and utilities before dewatering starts
Additional TestingContractor responsible for all additional ground investigation needed to design the system
Part 2 — Performance Requirements
Critical PerformancePiezometric level minimum 800mm below bottom of excavation at all times
OperationContinuous — no planned or unplanned interruption permitted during construction
Standby EquipmentFull standby set installed on site — immediately operational in case of primary system failure
Open-Sump PumpingProhibited — causes fines loss, soil piping, subgrade softening, and slope instability

Part 3 — Installation and Operation
WellsSpace at intervals required for sufficient dewatering — filters to prevent pumping of fine sands or silts
SumpsProvide sedimentation tanks and flow-control devices as required by DM authorities
ActivationPlace system in operation to lower water to required levels before excavating below groundwater level
Hydrostatic HeadReduce piezometric head in water-bearing strata to min 800mm below foundations, drains, sewers, other underground construction
DisposalDispose of removed water without endangering public health, property, or work in progress — approved discharge point only
Part 3 — Monitoring Protocol
Daily RecordsObserve and record daily elevation of groundwater and piezometric levels in all observation wells
Well RepairRepair or replace inactive/damaged observation wells within 24 hours
Survey BenchmarksResurvey benchmarks regularly — accurate log of elevations for comparison with original levels
Fines ObservationContinual observation to ensure subsurface soils are not being removed by dewatering operation
ReportsPrepare and submit daily observation reports to Engineer
Damage RepairPromptly repair any damages to adjacent facilities caused by dewatering
Site Technical Architect — Supervision Checkpoints
  • Verify delegated PE design is signed, sealed, and approved before any well installation
  • Confirm 800mm drawdown is achieved and recorded before each excavation stage progresses
  • Check daily piezometric logs are submitted on time — stop excavation if records missing
  • Verify standby pumps are physically present, installed, and operationally tested before bulk excavation
  • Confirm discharge point has authority approval — stop work if unapproved disposal location used
  • Monitor adjacent building settlement benchmarks — act immediately on anomalous readings
  • ITP hold point before each new excavation stage — sign off only when 800mm drawdown is confirmed
Dewatering failure during excavation is an immediate life-safety and structural risk. If the 800mm drawdown cannot be maintained at any point, all excavation stops immediately and the Engineer is notified. Do not allow work below an undrained water table under any circumstances. Standby pumps are mandatory — not optional.

Excavation Support and Protection
Design, installation, monitoring, and removal of all temporary excavation support systems: tiebacks, bracing, soldier piles, sheet piling, and lagging. Delegated-design by licensed PE. Every tieback load-tested. Continuous settlement monitoring mandatory throughout construction.
▾
Part 1 — Scope

Temporary excavation support and protection systems encompass all elements required to retain excavation faces and protect adjacent structures, utilities, roads, and walks during the enabling works and basement construction sequence. System is delegated-design: a licensed Professional Engineer designs, signs, seals, and holds full professional responsibility for all support elements. System remains in place until permanent structure independently resists all lateral earth and hydrostatic pressures.

Part 1 — Preinstallation Conference
  • Review geotechnical report — PE confirms design is based on actual site conditions
  • Review existing utilities and subsurface conditions — identify all services within influence zone
  • Review coordination for interruption, shutoff, capping, and continuation of utility services
  • Review proposed excavation sequence — confirm support system stages align
  • Review monitoring programme for excavation support and settlement
  • Review coordination with Section 071353 waterproofing sequence
  • Review abandonment or removal plan for support system
