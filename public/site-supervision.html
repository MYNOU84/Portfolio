◈ Zone 02 — Pre-Execution Gateway
02

Material Submittals

Verify the Substance Before Approving the Form

Material submittals are the first technical filter — verifying that what the contractor proposes to install matches the specification in substance, not just in name. Every submittal is reviewed against IFC drawings, technical datasheets, and physical samples. The output is binary: Approved, Approved with Comments, or Rejected. No intermediate status exists on a well-run site.

Key Actions
  • Verify IFC alignment — does the proposed material match the approved spec?
  • Review technical datasheets against performance requirements
  • Inspect physical samples under site-representative lighting conditions
  • Issue formal Approved / Approved with Comments / Rejected status
On-Site Reference IFC Alignment Technical Datasheets Physical Samples Approve / Comment / Reject

◈ Zone 02 — Pre-Execution Gateway
03

Shop Drawings

Where It Goes — Coordination & Alignment

Shop drawings translate the design intent into fabrication and installation reality. They show exact dimensions, fixing details, coordination with adjacent trades, and buildability logic. The architect's review focuses on IFC alignment and multidisciplinary coordination — not re-engineering the contractor's method, but confirming that what is drawn can be built without clashing or deviating from the approved design.

Key Actions
  • Verify drawings against IFC set — dimensions, levels, and clearances must match
  • Check coordination with structural, MEP, and facade drawings
  • Identify clash points before they become site RFIs
  • Confirm buildability — sequence must be physically achievable

◈ Zone 02 — Pre-Execution Gateway
04

Method Statement

How It Will Be Done — Safe, Sequenced, Controlled

The Method Statement defines the complete execution strategy before a single worker mobilizes. It must specify the step-by-step construction sequence, protective safety measures, required equipment and manpower, and integration with the Inspection and Test Plan (ITP). If the method is wrong, the output will be wrong — regardless of material quality. The architect reviews for compliance with specifications, safety, and sequence logic.

Key Actions
  • Verify construction sequence complies strictly with specifications
  • Review safety plan: protective measures and risk mitigation confirmed
  • Confirm equipment and manpower allocations are adequate for scope
  • Map ITP inspection checkpoints against method statement milestones

◈ Zone 02 — Pre-Execution Gateway
05

Mockup / Sample Approval

Test Once Before Repeating 1,000 Times

The physical mockup is the last filter before mass execution. It translates approved materials and methods into a real, inspectable result at full scale. This is the point where misalignments, finish discrepancies, unseen coordination issues, and workmanship shortfalls are exposed — before they multiply across the entire project. Rejecting a mockup costs days. Rejecting mass-installed work costs months and millions.

Key Actions
  • Inspect alignment: levels, joint widths, and setting-out accuracy
  • Verify finish quality: no defects, scratches, or contamination
  • Assess colour and texture against approved sample under natural and artificial light
  • Evaluate overall workmanship standard — this becomes the benchmark for all future work
On-Site Reference Alignment Finish Quality Color & Texture Workmanship Standard
WARNING: The actual look of the mockup is the only valid acceptance benchmark. Approved samples and drawings are inputs — the mockup is the final authority.

◈ Zone 03 — On-Site Reality
06

Site Execution

Execution Starts Only After All Gates Are Cleared

Site execution begins only after Steps 01–05 are formally closed. Workers execute against three fixed references simultaneously: the approved drawings, the approved method statement, and the approved mockup. Any deviation from these three references triggers an immediate IR hold and formal NCR. The Site Technical Architect maintains continuous presence at critical inspection points — execution is never a trusted, unsupervised process.

Key Actions
  • Confirm all gateway approvals (Steps 01–05) are formally closed before mobilization
  • Ensure approved drawings, method, and mockup are physically available on site
  • Activate IR cycle — no work proceeds beyond a hold point without inspection
  • Coordinate with QA/QC team for concurrent documentation

Project Handover & Closeout

Transfer Verified Quality — Close the Project with Evidence

Handover is not an event — it is the culmination of every inspection record, material approval, test report, and as-built update accumulated across the project lifecycle. Nothing is closed without formal confirmation.

Key Actions

  • Close all outstanding snagging items, NCRs, and inspection records before sign-off
  • Conduct final client walkthrough — every system demonstrated and accepted
  • Obtain formal handover certificate with client and authority sign-off

Zone 01 — Foundation
The Baseline

The Baseline establishes the non-negotiable ground truth for every subsequent site decision. It defines the geometric constraints from approved drawings, the qualitative demands from Project Specifications, and the financial and legal boundaries from the BOQ and Contract Conditions. Any execution deviating from this baseline without formal authority authorization is a critical non-conformance — full stop.

Zone 03 — On-Site Reality
On-Site Reality

Approved drawings and submittals mean nothing if site execution drifts unchecked. Zone 03 is where the Site Technical Architect operates at full intensity — inspection request cycles, daily coordination, quality control checks, and non-conformance response. Every IR closed is a quality record. Every NCR raised is a protection of the project baseline.

03
01
Inspection Request (IR) Cycle
Formal Hold Point Control
Before any work is concealed or covered, an Inspection Request is raised. The consultant verifies compliance with approved drawings, specifications, and method statements. No covering without a signed-off IR. Every IR is a permanent quality record.
Hold Point
02
Daily Coordination & RFI Management
Technical Queries Resolved on Record
Requests for Information are raised when site conditions conflict with drawings or when design clarification is needed. Every RFI must receive a formal written response before execution continues. Verbal instructions are not instructions — they are liabilities.
RFI Log Active
03
Non-Conformance Reports (NCR)
Deviation Captured, Tracked, Closed
When work deviates from approved standards, an NCR is raised immediately. The NCR defines the deviation, required rectification, and deadline. No NCR is closed without physical verification of correction. Open NCRs at handover are grounds for non-acceptance.
NCR Register

Method Statement Control

The Method Statement is the contractor's binding commitment to how work will be executed. It is not a formality — it is the technical and safety framework against which all execution is measured. Approved method statements define sequence, equipment, safety controls, and quality hold points. Deviations from approved methods are NCR-triggering events.

Zone 06 — Site Execution
Controlled Site Execution

Site execution is where design intent becomes permanent physical reality. Every installation, pour, weld, and finish applied to the building is irreversible without significant cost and programme impact. Zone 06 is the highest-intensity supervision phase — IR cycles run daily, quality checks are continuous, and deviations are captured and corrected before they propagate.

06
01
Progressive IR Cycle
Layer by Layer Verification
Each installation layer — substrate, waterproofing, screed, finish — is independently inspected and approved before the next layer commences. Substrate approval is the prerequisite for every subsequent trade. The IR cycle cannot be compressed or skipped.
Layer Control
02
QA/QC Surveillance
Continuous On-Site Monitoring
Daily site walks capture deviations before they become embedded defects. QA records — photographs, measurements, checklists — are filed for every inspection. A project's quality defence depends entirely on the consistency of its surveillance records.
QA Active

Zero Tolerance for Open NCRs

Zone 07 — Handover Sequence
The Handover Sequence

Handover is not an event — it is the result of every quality decision made from Zone 01 to Zone 06. A project handed over with open snagging items, incomplete documentation, or un-witnessed test results is not a completed project. The Handover Sequence closes every open loop before the client takes possession.

Zero Open Items at Handover

Snagging Complete

Documentation Package
As-Built · O&M Manuals · Test Reports
The complete documentation package — as-built drawings, operation & maintenance manuals, warranties, test & commissioning reports, and authority approvals — must be compiled, reviewed, and formally submitted. Incomplete documentation is incomplete handover.
Full Docs Package

Every System Demonstrated & Accepted

Section 07
Glossary
IFC — Issued for Construction
The formally approved drawing set released to the contractor for physical execution on site. Supersedes all previous revisions.
Shop Drawing
Contractor-produced detailed fabrication and installation drawing submitted for consultant review and approval before work commences.
Material Submittal
Formal submission of proposed materials, products, and systems for consultant review against project specifications and authority requirements.
MIR — Material Inspection Request
Formal request raised by the contractor for the consultant to verify that materials delivered to site match the approved submittal.
IR / WIR — Inspection / Work Inspection Request
Formal request by the contractor for the consultant to inspect installed works at a defined hold point before the next stage commences.
ITP — Inspection and Test Plan
A document that defines hold points, witness points, and record points for a specific work activity, controlling when and how inspections occur.
Method Statement
Contractor's documented plan describing how a specific work activity will be executed — sequence, equipment, safety measures, and quality controls.
RFI — Request for Information
Formal query raised by the contractor to the consultant seeking clarification on design intent, specification requirements, or drawing discrepancies.
NCR — Non-Conformance Report
Formal record issued when work, material, or process deviates from the approved standard. Requires formal response and verified rectification before closure.
SI — Site Instruction
Formal written instruction issued by the consultant directing the contractor to take a specific action on site. Creates a contractual record.
CDE — Common Data Environment
The agreed information management platform through which all project documents, drawings, and models are shared, reviewed, and approved.
O&M Manual
Operation and Maintenance Manual — a complete guide for the client and facilities management team on operating, maintaining, and servicing all installed systems.
As-Built Drawing
Final drawing set updated to reflect the exact as-constructed condition of all works, submitted as part of the project handover documentation package.

Professional Position Statement

A Senior Technical Architect with architectural package coordination and site supervision interface acts as the technical bridge between design documentation and construction execution. The role controls the quality and compliance of architectural information, coordinates package approvals, resolves technical issues with structure and MEP, supports site inspections, monitors NCR / RFI / SI closure, and ensures that architectural works are executed in accordance with approved drawings, specifications, Dubai Building Code, UAE Fire and Life Safety requirements, Al Sa'fat requirements, and the project QA/QC procedures.

This position is not three full-time roles combined into one person. The core function is Senior Technical Architecture, supported by architectural package coordination responsibilities and a site supervision interface.
