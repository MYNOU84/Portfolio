Contract Documents & Specifications

The Legal & Technical Foundation

The signed contract and project specifications define every quality standard, material grade, workmanship requirement, and performance benchmark for the project. The Site Technical Architect must have command of every clause before a single trade mobilizes. This document is the non-negotiable ground truth.

Key Actions
  • Review and extract all specification clauses relevant to each trade
  • Cross-reference authority-approved drawings with contract drawings
  • Identify performance benchmarks: strengths, tolerances, test requirements
  • Map BOQ quantities against material specs before procurement begins

Material Submittals

Verify the Substance Before Approving the Form

Material submittals are the first technical filter — verifying that what the contractor proposes to install matches the specification in substance, not just in name. Every submittal is reviewed against IFC drawings, technical datasheets, and physical samples. The output is binary: Approved, Approved with Comments, or Rejected. No intermediate status exists on a well-run site.

Key Actions
  • Verify IFC alignment — does the proposed material match the approved spec?
  • Review technical datasheets against performance requirements
  • Inspect physical samples under site-representative lighting conditions
  • Issue formal Approved / Approved with Comments / Rejected status
On-Site Reference IFC Alignment Technical Datasheets Physical Samples Approve / Comment / Reject

◈ Zone 02 — Pre-Execution Gateway

Shop Drawings

Where It Goes — Coordination & Alignment

Shop drawings translate the design intent into fabrication and installation reality. They show exact dimensions, fixing details, coordination with adjacent trades, and buildability logic. The architect's review focuses on IFC alignment and multidisciplinary coordination — not re-engineering the contractor's method, but confirming that what is drawn can be built without clashing or deviating from the approved design.

Key Actions
  • Verify drawings against IFC set — dimensions, levels, and clearances must match
  • Check coordination with structural, MEP, and facade drawings
  • Identify clash points before they become site RFIs
  • Confirm buildability — sequence must be physically achievable
On-Site Reference Shop Drawings IFC Alignment Coordination Buildability

◈ Zone 02 — Pre-Execution Gateway
04

Method Statement

How It Will Be Done — Safe, Sequenced, Controlled

The Method Statement defines the complete execution strategy before a single worker mobilizes. It must specify the step-by-step construction sequence, protective safety measures, required equipment and manpower, and integration with the Inspection and Test Plan (ITP). If the method is wrong, the output will be wrong — regardless of material quality. The architect reviews for compliance with specifications, safety, and sequence logic.

Key Actions
  • Verify construction sequence complies strictly with specifications
  • Review safety plan: protective measures and risk mitigation confirmed
  • Confirm equipment and manpower allocations are adequate for scope
  • Map ITP inspection checkpoints against method statement milestones
On-Site Reference Sequence of Work Safety & Protection Equipment & Manpower ITP / Inspection Points

◈ Zone 02 — Pre-Execution Gateway
05

Mockup / Sample Approval

Test Once Before Repeating 1,000 Times

The physical mockup is the last filter before mass execution. It translates approved materials and methods into a real, inspectable result at full scale. This is the point where misalignments, finish discrepancies, unseen coordination issues, and workmanship shortfalls are exposed — before they multiply across the entire project. Rejecting a mockup costs days. Rejecting mass-installed work costs months and millions.

Key Actions
  • Inspect alignment: levels, joint widths, and setting-out accuracy
  • Verify finish quality: no defects, scratches, or contamination
  • Assess colour and texture against approved sample under natural and artificial light
  • Evaluate overall workmanship standard — this becomes the benchmark for all future work
On-Site Reference Alignment Finish Quality Color & Texture Workmanship Standard
WARNING: The actual look of the mockup is the only valid acceptance benchmark. Approved samples and drawings are inputs — the mockup is the final authority.

◈ Zone 03 — On-Site Reality
06

Site Execution

Execution Starts Only After All Gates Are Cleared

Site execution begins only after Steps 01–05 are formally closed. Workers execute against three fixed references simultaneously: the approved drawings, the approved method statement, and the approved mockup. Any deviation from these three references triggers an immediate IR hold and formal NCR. The Site Technical Architect maintains continuous presence at critical inspection points — execution is never a trusted, unsupervised process.

Key Actions
  • Confirm all gateway approvals (Steps 01–05) are formally closed before mobilization
  • Ensure approved drawings, method, and mockup are physically available on site
  • Activate IR cycle — no work proceeds beyond a hold point without inspection
  • Coordinate with QA/QC team for concurrent documentation

◈ Zone 04 — The Handover Sequence
07

Project Handover & Closeout

Transfer Verified Quality — Close the Project with Evidence

Handover is not an event — it is the culmination of every inspection record, material approval, test report, and as-built update accumulated across the project lifecycle. The Site Technical Architect is responsible for compiling a complete, legally defensible handover package and ensuring client sign-off is based on verified physical and documentary evidence. Nothing is closed without formal confirmation.

Key Actions
  • Compile as-built drawings, O&M manuals, warranties, and certifications into a complete handover package
  • Close all outstanding snagging items, NCRs, and inspection records before sign-off
  • Conduct final client walkthrough — every system demonstrated and accepted
  • Obtain formal handover certificate with client and authority sign-off

✓ Project Closed

Zone 01 — Foundation
The Baseline

The Baseline establishes the non-negotiable ground truth for every subsequent site decision. It defines the geometric constraints from approved drawings, the qualitative demands from Project Specifications, and the financial and legal boundaries from the BOQ and Contract Conditions. Any execution deviating from this baseline without formal authority authorization is a critical non-conformance — full stop.

Pre-Execution Gateways

Pre-execution gateways separate substance from form. Each gateway is a mandatory checkpoint — no physical work commences on site until all relevant gateways are closed with formal approval. A single unapproved submittal or unchecked shop drawing released to site represents an open liability that cascades through every subsequent trade.
